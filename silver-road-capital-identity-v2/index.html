The firm is the place where different roads meet.

Clients come to Silver Road from different markets, with different capital and different goals. What the firm does is bring them to the same point and move them forward together. No peer of this size can say that across Israel, North America and Europe at once. That is the idea the identity has to carry.

The eight paths stay. Everything around them is rebuilt.

Today the firm reads its mark as many paths leading to growth. That story is right, and it is the firm's own. The drawing is what holds it back: fine lines inside a gradient, a second colour and a second voice in the name.

  • The story is now the logo

    The eight paths are the whole mark, large and countable, not a detail inside a blue dome.

  • One colour, any material

    No gradient and no hairlines. It prints, engraves, embosses and foils exactly as drawn.

  • It holds when small

    A dedicated small cut keeps the paths open in a browser tab, an app icon and a deal list.

  • It becomes a system

    The same paths build covers, web pages, photo frames and figures, so every document is recognisably Silver Road before the name is read.

Eight paths rise from different starting points and become one road at the horizon.

The disc is the world the firm works in. The paths begin wide apart at its edge, where clients begin, and draw together as they climb. At the top they are a single line. Read from above it is a road; read from below it is growth.

Our proposed reading for the firm to adopt or adjust: the paths are the markets and partners that meet through Silver Road. We have not assigned each path a name, because the firm has not, and the number is its own.

Below 40 pixels a simplified cut with fewer, heavier paths takes over, so the shape never closes up.

Drawn, not illustrated.

One circle. One horizon line across its top. One point on that line. Every path is an arc of a circle that passes through that point, and the widths step up by a fixed ratio from the centre outward.

Because it is geometry, it can be redrawn at any size by anyone, from a signage contractor to a pitch-book template, and it will always be the same mark.

A new voice: one serif with authority, one sans with precision.

Raleway is a light geometric sans. It is friendly, and it is everywhere. The houses the firm sits beside speak in type with history. The proposal moves the firm's name and its headlines to a serif, and keeps text and figures in a clear modern sans.

Newsreader Italic

Newsreader, for the name, headlines and large figures. A contemporary serif drawn for reading on screens and in print, with true italics and lining numerals.

Hanken Grotesk

Hanken Grotesk, for text, tables, navigation and small labels. Open, even and legible at small sizes.

The palette: the firm's blue, made deeper and given real silver.
