A new logo and palette for Silver Road Capital Group

One mark, one wordmark and nine colours, drawn to the standard of the leading global advisory houses. Identity proposal, November 2026.

What the current identity does

The current mark relies on seven fine lines and a gradient, so its detail closes up at small sizes and it cannot be engraved, embossed or printed in one colour as drawn.

The wordmark sets Silver Road in chrome-effect capitals and Capital in widely spaced lowercase in a second colour, two voices where the leading advisory houses use one.

The palette holds eleven colours, including three bright blues and two golds beside the silver, so no single colour is recognisably the firm's.

The current palette, eleven colours

The mark

The idea the firm already owns is kept: a silver road running to the horizon. The disc is the ground the firm works across, and the two lines are the edges of a road that starts wide at the viewer and meets at one point on the far edge. Seven lines and a gradient become two lines and one colour, built from a circle and straight edges, so the mark holds from a favicon to a building sign and can be foiled, engraved or embossed. The name is set in Raleway, the firm's own typeface, in capitals at a single size, with Silver Road in a medium weight and Capital in regular.

The palette

Eleven colours become nine, each with one job. Road Navy carries the firm: it is the logo colour and the dark ground, and it is deep enough to sit beside the navy of the established houses. Silver is now a cool, blue-leaning grey that reads as metal next to navy, and it is specified as foil or Pantone 877 C wherever print allows. Gold is retired, because a second metal competes with the name. The three bright blues reduce to one accent, Horizon Blue, taken from the blue of the current logo, deepened so it passes as text, and used sparingly.
